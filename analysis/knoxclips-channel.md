## Visual formula (consistent across all 4)

- **Hybrid look:** flat, black-outlined 2D characters (bean-shaped bodies, stick limbs, dot eyes) placed in **3D toon-shaded sets** with real perspective, depth and cast shadows.
- **Recurring character design:** the same bean body, given identity by one or two props (monocle and green suit, red bobble hat and star sweater, laurel crown and sash). Supporting animals recur too (chicken, cat).
- **Faces carry the emotion:** wide eyes and O-mouth for shock, sweat drops, squinting smiles. Expressions are pushed big enough to read on a phone.
- **Infographics live inside the world:** in the pirate video, a speedometer reading "20 KNOTS" and a sign reading "15-25 CREW" sit in the scene like real objects, not as flat overlays.
- **Captions** are identical in every video: one or two words at a time, bold white text with a dark outline, centred in the lower-middle third.

## Lighting, colour and shadow

- **One palette per video,** chosen for the topic:
  - Pirates: pink-sunset sky against saturated blue sea (warm against cool).
  - Rich vs Poor: gold and green for wealth, grey-beige for poverty, blue-purple night with an orange fire (measured: saturation drops ~5× at "poor"; night brightness halves).
  - Greed: orange desert sand with **purple/violet shadows** (complementary colours).
  - Shooting star: blue-purple night with warm string lights and a red hat accent.
- **Coloured shadows, not grey ones:** shadows are pushed toward purple/blue while key light stays warm. That warm-key / cool-shadow split gives the "premium" look and keeps contrast readable without harsh blacks.
- **One directional sun key** with long, soft-edged cast shadows that ground the 2D characters in the 3D sets. Shadow length is also used to show time passing ("3 days later").
- **Contrast is moderate and high-key in daylight,** so the frame never gets muddy; night scenes drop overall brightness but keep faces lit.

## Camera, cuts and effects

From the full analysis of "Rich And Poor #2", plus what the stills of the others suggest:
- **About one shot per 1–1.5 s,** with the picture changing on nearly every caption phrase.
- **Transitions are mostly camera moves,** not hard cuts: whip-pans with motion blur, tilts to the sky, fast push-ins, and dissolves for time or mood shifts.
- **Shot variety:** wide establishing shots (ship at sea, rows of barrels, soldiers in the desert) alternate with close-ups on a reacting face.
- **Effects:** particle streams (coins), objects flying away (houses, fields), speed lines (shooting star), small idle loops (shivering, flickering fire, a tumbleweed).

## Script style

- Fable or explainer told in **short present-tense statements**.
- Lists of three with a one-word punch ("Every coin, every house, every field: gone.").
- Repetition ("Everyone is poor. Everyone is hungry.").
- Time-jump beats ("3 days later,").
- Groups set up for a comparison ("The first group…") so the moral lands in the contrast.
- About 175 words per minute.
- Caption fragments from the stills ("exactly", "it relatively", "with the exact", "They follow", "and a bright", "the sky.", "time?") show the same rhythm elsewhere: one or two words per caption, explanation-heavy, with questions asked in the narration.

## Takeaways if you want to make this style

1. Pick a **fable or "why" question with a clear moral or answer**. That is the format that hit 3.5M; vague self-help titles flopped.
2. Use the **2D-characters-in-3D-sets** look, with props as character identity.
3. Grade **by emotion**: saturated for good fortune, desaturated for loss, dark and cool for consequence. Keep shadows tinted purple/blue.
4. **Cut or move the camera every ~1 s,** mostly with whip-pans, tilts and push-ins rather than plain cuts.
5. Show **one or two caption words at a time,** in big white outlined text in the lower-middle third.
6. Write **short punchy lines,** lists of three, repetition, and visible time jumps.
